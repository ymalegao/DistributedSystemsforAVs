### Round 1: The Genesis (Creating the First QC)
**Context:** The system has just booted up. There is no previous QC. The Waiting Map is empty `{}`.

**1. The Event (Simultaneous Arrival)**
* Cars A, B, C, and D all connect to the network.
* They all send `JOIN` requests.
* **Car B (Malicious)** sends a modified request payload: `JOIN (Timestamp: 1990, Priority: HIGH)`. (Spoiler: This won't work).

**2. The Proposal (Leader A)**
* Car A (Leader) packages all requests into **Batch #1**.
* Proposal: `[Req(A), Req(B), Req(C), Req(D)]`.
* *Note:* B's malicious payload is included in the batch just like everyone else's.

**3. The Execution (appExecuteBatch)**
Every node (A, B, C, D) runs the exact same code on this batch.

* **Logic Step 1 (Gather):** The code identifies 4 joiners.
* **Logic Step 2 (Assign Time):** The code iterates through the joiners.
    * `map.put(A, 1)`
    * `map.put(B, 1)` -> **Defense:** The code ignores B's "1990" payload and hardcodes `1`.
    * `map.put(C, 1)`
    * `map.put(D, 1)`
* **Logic Step 3 (Tie-Break):** Who goes first?
    * Max Wait? All are 1.
    * Min ID? A is 0, B is 1... **Winner is A.**
* **Logic Step 4 (Auto-Eject):**
    * `map.remove(A)`
    * `lastLeaver = A`

**4. The Result (QC #1)**
* The resulting state is `{B:1, C:1, D:1}`.
* Nodes A, C, D sign this state.
* Node B (Byzantine) signs a *fake* state `{B:100, C:1, D:1}` because it wants to be next.
* **Consensus:** A, C, D match. 3 votes vs 1. The honest state is committed.
* **Physical Action:** Car A receives the "Green Light" reply and drives away.

### Intermission: The Stateless Join & The Attack
**Context:** Round 1 is over. The committed state is `{B:1, C:1, D:1}`.
**Event:** **Car E** arrives. It knows nothing.

**1. Discovery (The "Hello")**
* Car E broadcasts: "I need the state!"

**2. The Attack (State Transfer)**
* **Honest C & D send:** `QC_State: {B:1, C:1, D:1}` (Signed by A, C, D).
* **Malicious B sends:** `QC_State: {B:100, C:1, D:1}` (Signed only by B).

**3. Verification (Car E's Defense)**
* Car E looks at C & D's packet: "I see 3 valid signatures (A, C, D). This matches the threshold."
* Car E looks at B's packet: "I see only 1 signature (B). This is invalid."
* **Action:** Car E installs `{B:1, C:1, D:1}`. Car E now knows B is actually the priority (wait=1), but B hasn't waited 100 rounds.

### Round 2: The Join & The Departure
**Context:** Current State `{B:1, C:1, D:1}`.
**Leader:** Let's say the leadership rotates to **Car C** (since A left).

**1. The Event**
* Car E sends `JOIN`.
* Car B (Malicious) sends `LEAVE` (Trying to get out).
* Car C & D just wait.

**2. The Proposal (Leader C)**
* Batch #2: `[Req(E), Req(B)]`.

**3. The Execution (appExecuteBatch)**
Every node runs the logic on Batch #2.

* **Logic Step 1 (Aging - The "Tick"):**
    * Everyone currently in the map gets +1.
    * Map updates: `{B:2, C:2, D:2}`.
* **Logic Step 2 (Process Join E):**
    * E is a new joiner.
    * `map.put(E, 1)`.
    * Current Map: `{B:2, C:2, D:2, E:1}`.
* **Logic Step 3 (Process Leave B):**
    * B requested to leave.
    * **Check:** Is B the winner?
        * Max Wait: 2 (B, C, D).
        * Tie-Break: B (ID 1) < C (ID 2) < D (ID 3).
        * **Winner is B.**
    * **Action:** Allow B to leave. `map.remove(B)`.

**4. The Result (QC #2)**
* Final State: `{C:2, D:2, E:1}`.
* **Physical Action:** Car B receives "Green Light". Car E receives "Ack, you are in queue at wait time 1".

### How the Byzantine Node B Failed
1.  **Lying about Time (Round 1):** Failed because the code ignored the input timestamp and hardcoded `1`.
2.  **Lying about State (Intermission):** Failed because E required a Quorum Certificate ($2f+1$ signatures), and B couldn't forge the signatures of C and D.
3.  **Lying about Priority (Round 2):** Even if B didn't want to leave yet (to block traffic), the logic determined B was the winner. If B *refused* to send the `LEAVE` request, the system would just keep aging everyone `{B:3, C:3...}` until B sends the request or is kicked out by a timeout policy.

### Scenario: Round 3 - The "Newbie" Byzantine Leader
**Context:**
* **State at start of Round 3:** `{C:2, D:2, E:1}`.
* **The Priority:** Car C (Wait time 2, Lowest ID).
* **The Leader:** **Car E** (The New Joiner) is elected Leader.
* **The Threat:** Car E is Byzantine. It wants to **jump the line** and leave immediately, screwing over C and D.

Here is the step-by-step failure of Car E's attack.

#### Attack 1: The "Forbidden Proposal"
**Car E (Leader)** wants to propose that *it* leaves.
**Car C** sends a `LEAVE` request (since C knows it's C's turn).

1.  **The Proposal:**
    Leader E ignores C's request. Leader E creates a Batch containing only its own request: `[Request: LEAVE(E)]`.
    * *Leader E hopes the others will just blindly sign whatever it sends.*

2.  **The Execution (at Replica C and Replica D):**
    Nodes C and D receive the Batch `[LEAVE(E)]`. They run `appExecuteBatch`:
    * **Step A (Tick):** Everyone ages +1. State becomes `{C:3, D:3, E:2}`.
    * **Step B (Identify Winner):**
        * $C=3$, $D=3$, $E=2$.
        * **Winner is C.**
    * **Step C (Process Request):**
        * The request asks to execute `LEAVE(E)`.
        * **The Guardrail:** `if (requester == winner)`.
        * Is $E == C$? **NO.**
        * **Action:** The request is marked "REJECTED" (or treated as a No-Op). The state remains `{C:3, D:3, E:2}`.

3.  **The Result (QC #3):**
    * The Quorum Certificate produced for Round 3 is **{C:3, D:3, E:2}**.
    * **E is NOT removed.**
    * **E does NOT get the green light.**

**Outcome:** The system remained **Safe**. The Byzantine Leader tried to violate the fairness rule, but the honest majority (C and D) enforced the rule mathematically. E simply wasted a round.

---

#### Attack 2: The "Censorship" (The Stall)
**Car E (Leader)** realizes it can't jump the line. So instead, it decides to **block C** by refusing to include C's `LEAVE` request in the batch.

1.  **The Proposal:**
    Leader E sends an **Empty Batch** `[]`. Or a batch with only unrelated `JOIN` requests.

2.  **The Execution:**
    Nodes C and D execute the Empty Batch.
    * **Step A (Tick):** Everyone ages +1. State `{C:3, D:3, E:2}`.
    * **Step B:** No leave requests to process.
    * **Result:** State remains `{C:3, D:3, E:2}`.

3.  **The Liveness Fix (The Timeout):**
    * Car C sees that its `LEAVE` request was ignored by Leader E.
    * Car C starts a **Timer**.
    * If Leader E continues to produce empty blocks or ignore C's request for too long (e.g., 100ms), Car C broadcasts a **STOP_VIEW** (View Change) message.
    * Car D sees the same thing.
    * Once $2f+1$ nodes shout "STOP," **Leader E is fired.**
    * **Leadership rotates to Car C.**

**Outcome:** A Byzantine Leader can **delay** traffic (Liveness attack), but it cannot **crash** cars or violate priority (Safety/Fairness attack). The protocol eventually removes the malicious leader.

---

#### Attack 3: The "Equivocation" (The Split)
**Car E (Leader)** tries to confuse the network.
* To **Car C**, E sends: "Batch = [LEAVE(C)]" (Act nice).
* To **Car D**, E sends: "Batch = [LEAVE(D)]" (Cause a crash).

1.  **The BFT Defense (The Hash Check):**
    * This happens *inside* the BFT-SMART library (Consensus Phase).
    * Car C says: "I received a Batch with Hash **X**."
    * Car D says: "I received a Batch with Hash **Y**."
    * **Mismatch!**
    * Since C and D cannot agree on *what* the Leader proposed, they **DO NOT SIGN** a QC.

**Outcome:** No QC is generated. Round 3 fails. The timeout triggers. Leader E is fired as a liar.

### Summary of the Defense

1.  **The Leader is a Clerk, not a Dictator:** The Leader only suggests *inputs* (The Batch). The Leader cannot dictate the *output* (The Map).
2.  **The Replicas are Judges:** C and D independently calculate who *should* leave. If the Leader's input doesn't result in a valid exit according to *their* calculation, the exit doesn't happen.
3.  **The Timer is the Sheriff:** If the Leader refuses to do its job (process requests), the Timer fires the Leader.

So, even if the newest, most untrustworthy car becomes the Leader, your intersection is safe.
